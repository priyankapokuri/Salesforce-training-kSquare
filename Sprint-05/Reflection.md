# Reflection – Chapter 5: Building Business Logic with Apex

## What Worked Well

This sprint helped me understand that software development begins with understanding business requirements rather than writing code immediately.

Designing service classes before implementation made the overall architecture easier to understand.

---

## Most Interesting Part

The most interesting concept was realizing that an Apex class is not just a programming construct—it represents a specific business responsibility.

Thinking in terms of responsibilities instead of syntax changed the way I approach software design.

---

## Biggest Challenge

The biggest challenge was identifying business rules and deciding which service should own each responsibility.

Separating responsibilities between StudentService, JobService, and ApplicationService required careful analysis before implementation.

---

## Lessons Learned

- Understand the business before writing code.
- Business logic gives intelligence to software.
- One service should have one responsibility.
- Methods should represent business activities.
- Good architecture improves maintainability.
- Clear naming improves readability.

---

## Future Improvements

If this project continued, I would:

- Add reusable helper methods.
- Improve exception handling.
- Create comprehensive Apex Test Classes.
- Introduce Trigger Handler architecture.
- Optimize business validations for scalability.

---

## Today I Learned

> Software is valuable not because it stores data, but because it consistently makes correct business decisions based on business rules.

---

## Areas for Improvement

- Improve architectural thinking before implementation.
- Practice identifying business responsibilities.
- Design smaller, reusable methods.
- Strengthen Apex service design skills.

---

## Sprint Summary

Chapter 4 introduced the foundation of enterprise software development by focusing on business logic, architecture, and service-oriented design.

The most important lesson was understanding that technology follows business understanding. Apex is simply the language used to implement well-designed business responsibilities, while architecture and problem analysis come first.
