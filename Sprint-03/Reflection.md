# Reflection – Salesforce Interview Readiness Bootcamp (Day 3)

## What Worked Well

Today's assignment helped me understand how Salesforce provides multiple automation tools to solve different business problems.

Instead of immediately choosing Apex, I learned to evaluate whether a Validation Rule or Flow could solve the requirement more efficiently.

---

## Most Interesting Part

The most interesting concept was understanding how Flow, Validation Rules, and Apex work together during record processing.

Learning to choose the right automation tool rather than writing unnecessary code was an important shift in my thinking.

---

## Biggest Challenge

The biggest challenge was deciding which business requirements should be implemented using Flow and which required Apex.

Understanding the execution order of Salesforce automation also required careful study.

---

## Lessons Learned

- Validation Rules prevent invalid data from being saved.
- Record-Triggered Flows automate business processes without code.
- Apex should be reserved for complex business logic.
- Declarative automation improves maintainability.
- Selecting the correct automation tool is an important interview skill.

---

## Future Improvements

If this project were expanded further, I would:

- Introduce reusable Flow subflows.
- Improve Apex exception handling.
- Implement a Trigger Handler framework.
- Add Apex Test Classes.
- Build more advanced approval processes.

---

## Today I Learned

> A good Salesforce developer does not write Apex for every requirement. They first evaluate whether Validation Rules or Flows can solve the problem before writing code.

---

## Areas for Improvement

- Gain more experience with Flow Builder.
- Learn advanced Flow features such as Scheduled Paths and Subflows.
- Practice complex Validation Rule formulas.
- Improve understanding of Salesforce execution order.

---

## Sprint Summary

Today's assignment strengthened my understanding of Salesforce automation by combining Validation Rules, Record-Triggered Flows, and Apex into a single application.

The biggest takeaway was learning to choose the right automation tool based on business requirements instead of relying on Apex for every solution. This approach leads to cleaner, more maintainable, and scalable Salesforce applications.
